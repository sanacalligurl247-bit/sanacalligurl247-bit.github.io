Worked example: a shop fielding about 100 minutes of answered calls a month pays roughly $345–$500/mo at the published per-minute rates above (100 min × $3.45–$5.00), before any overage — and that buys message-taking only. The question isn’t whether a human voice is nice. It’s whether message-taking is the job you actually need done.

Fairness note — where an answering service genuinely wins

A good human operator wins on nuance: a distressed caller, a complicated situation, an existing customer with history, a conversation that needs judgment and warmth in real time. If your calls are complex, emotional, or mostly from people who already know your shop, a person answering live can be the better tool — and services like Ruby and Smith.ai have earned real reputations doing it. Eve wins when the typical missed call is a new customer with a straightforward need who will text back but won’t wait — and when you also need the get-found work an answering service simply doesn’t do.
