The coverage math nobody puts in the job post

A week has 168 hours. One full-time receptionist covers 40 of them — about 24%. The other 128 hours include every evening, every weekend, every lunch break, sick day, and vacation.

True round-the-clock coverage takes about 4 full-time equivalents (168 ÷ 40 = 4.2, before breaks and time off) — roughly $196,000–$216,000 a year fully loaded at the figures above. Almost no owner-run shop needs or buys that. But it’s the honest yardstick: missed calls don’t only happen during business hours — for home services, a large share happen precisely when nobody is in the office.

Eve covers the missed-call moment itself — day or night, usually texting back in under about a minute, asking the 3 qualifying questions, and emailing you the summary. She does nothing else on this page’s list. That narrowness is the point.

Fairness note — where a receptionist genuinely wins

A receptionist wins almost everywhere except cost and coverage hours. They greet customers in person, handle paperwork, juggle the schedule, calm an upset caller with real judgment, and become part of your shop’s reputation. If you have a storefront or office with walk-in traffic and the volume to keep a person busy all day, hire the person — Eve is not a replacement for that job, and this page isn’t claiming she is. Eve wins for the owner-run shop whose only problem is the phone ringing out on the job site: too small for a $4,100–$4,500/mo hire, losing real jobs at 7 PM and on Saturdays.
