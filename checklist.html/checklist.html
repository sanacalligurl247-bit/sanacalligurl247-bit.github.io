The AI Visibility Checklist

9 things ChatGPT checks before it recommends a local business

More customers now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity "who's a good painter / plumber / HVAC company near me?" instead of scrolling Google. The AI names a few businesses and skips the rest. This checklist is how to check which side of that line your business is on — about 10 minutes, no tools to buy. Eight of these are things the AI looks at; the ninth decides whether the call becomes a job.

1. Ask the AI yourself.
Open ChatGPT and ask: "Who is the best [your trade] in [your city]?" Then ask it on Gemini and Perplexity too. Are you named at all? Which competitors are named instead? Write down what you see — this is your starting line.

2. Your Google Business Profile is complete.
AI assistants lean heavily on it. Business name, categories, services, hours, photos, service area — every field filled in. An unfinished profile reads to AI like a business that may not be active.

3. Your name, address, and phone match everywhere.
The exact same business name, address, and phone number on your website, Google, Facebook, Yelp, and directories. Mismatches make AI unsure it is looking at one business, and unsure businesses don't get recommended.

4. Reviews: enough of them, recent, and answered.
Count and freshness matter, and so does the owner replying. A business with 80 reviews, the newest three years old and none answered, looks asleep next to a competitor with 40 fresh, answered ones.

5. Your website says what you do and where, in plain words.
AI reads text. If your site is mostly photos, it cannot tell what services you offer or which towns you cover. One plain paragraph per service, with the towns named, beats a beautiful gallery.

6. Prices — or at least price ranges — are published.
AI loves to answer "how much does it cost?" Businesses whose prices it can find get quoted to customers. Businesses with no prices anywhere get skipped more often — when the AI can't find yours, it quotes a pricing guide or a national chain instead.

7. Your hours agree with each other.
Hours on Google vs. hours on your website vs. hours on Facebook. If they disagree, AI trusts none of them — and a customer told the wrong hours is a customer lost.

8. There is a way to reach you after hours that isn't a ringing phone.
Most calls to local trades come when the owner is on a job. A text option, a form that promises a fast reply, anything that answers when you can't. A missed call with no answer usually becomes the next company's job within the hour.

9. Your name shows up in more than one place.
AI names the businesses it keeps seeing: directories, local articles, Facebook, Nextdoor, supplier pages. One website alone is a whisper; the same name in five places is a reputation.

Scoring yourself

Count the items you could honestly check off.

7–9: AI can find you and explain you. Your gap is probably answering speed.
4–6: You're half-visible — named sometimes, skipped often.
0–3: To AI, you mostly don't exist yet, no matter how good the work is.
