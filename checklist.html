The Top-10 Check

Are you on the list Google hands your customers?

When someone needs a painter, plumber, or HVAC company, they ask Google — and Google answers with a short list: the map results and the AI answer at the top. If you're not on that list, you're not in the running, no matter how good the work is. This checklist shows where you stand.

1. Run the top-10 question yourself.
Search Google for "top 10 [your trade] in [your county]" and read the AI answer and the map list. Are you named? Where do you sit — and who is above you?

2. Your Google Business Profile is complete.
Business name, categories, services, hours, photos, service area — every field filled in. An unfinished profile reads like a business that may be inactive.

3. Your name, address, and phone match everywhere.
The same business name, address, and phone on your website, Google, Facebook, Yelp, and directories. Mismatches make Google unsure it is looking at one business — and unsure businesses don't make the list.

4. Reviews: enough of them, recent, and answered.
Count, freshness, and owner replies all matter. Eighty reviews with the newest three years old and none answered look asleep next to forty fresh, answered ones.

5. Your website says what you do and where, in plain words.
If your site is mostly photos, Google can't tell what services you offer or which towns you cover. One plain paragraph per service, towns named, beats a beautiful gallery.

6. Your hours agree with each other.
Hours on Google vs. hours on your website vs. hours on Facebook. If they disagree, Google trusts none of them — and a customer told the wrong hours is a customer lost.

7. Your name shows up in more than one place.
Directories, local articles, Facebook, Nextdoor, supplier pages. Google names the businesses it keeps seeing. One website alone is a whisper; the same name in five places is a reputation.

8. There is a way to reach you after hours that isn't a ringing phone.
Most calls come when the owner is on a job. A text option, a form that promises a fast reply — anything that answers when you can't. A missed call becomes the next company's job within the hour.

Scoring yourself

Count what you could honestly check off.

7–8: Google can find you and explain you. Your gap is probably answering speed.
4–6: You're half-visible — on some lists, skipped on others.
0–3: To Google, you mostly don't exist yet, no matter how good the work is.
